Riding the pain

When pain becomes a friend Reaching out in the night, Sending you a hello With the morning coffee, Holding your hand As your appetite vanishes At lunchtime. Kissing you goodnight, Disturbing the bed Insomniac. There comes a moment You finally admit, Echoing the doctors, That he is here To stay. How many pills can you take? You learn To dissociate, Cut feeling from the body, Become numb, Transcended To accommodate your familiar. You ride the pain Until it becomes pleasure In your ability To withstand, Feeling stronger Than others, Unburdened. I took this path, I admit Tempted by the absence Of that inner crumpling. I embraced My foe, For the world offered No alternative. Complacency And tiredness Made me mute the bells Of warning. Shutting down feeling Feels nice For we all chase The absence of pain. We can lose sight That pain, A symptom, Is also A cause Wrecking the body, Untamed, Unstoppable. It reigns, driving you Toward transcendence Toward death As you leave the body That witnessed Your trial. I too came close To the limit. Battered wounds, Unhealed scars, Damaged I will sing the song Of an orphan Taught to accept What could not Be prevented, What was left Neglected. My nature, A phoenix Drenched in blood Crippled, Destitute. Will you answer the call? Or blame the fiery Creature for burning On its own?

Catching salmon

The water closes around my toes gently exploring as if it hasn’t decided about me yet I let it settle let the surface smooth over the fact that I’m here There’s a scent rising salty but heavier than that something warm beneath it that lingers in the back of my throat I shift ever so slightly enough to be noticed enough to rub the budding yearning Inside I still myself The waiting stretches tightens until my body is listening for something I cannot name And then- a trace So faint I almost miss it a brush that could be nothing except it is What I want I freeze In anticipation and feel it stay close not gone just waiting for me to reach for it This time I don’t pull away I let my legs sink deeper Sliding into the water I shiver When the cool Touches my warmth And with it a slow pull tightening at my chest as if something unseen has found me drawing gently then deeper a quiet, rhythmic taking that leaves me aching into it Another pass closer now no longer hesitant inquisitive sliding between before slipping away My breath shifts The pull returns stronger now lingering as if each movement below is answered above by that same soft insistent drawing Something in me gives I stop pretending it’s accidental Let the water move how it wants let whatever lives beneath find me again again each time a little longer a little less uncertain closer tighter I chase it Welcome it I stay open feeling the contact come and return and come back again until the waiting is gone and there’s only the quiet pull of letting it happen

Blackberry bush

Everyone said: don’t go into the tangle where daylight gets caught and stays A blackberry bush like a jungle grew steady through our days sweet in July, ink-dark by August perfume and promised gold every time I came back marked by it the sweetness comes to the bold And the blackberries ripen and the blackberries teach the hearts how to reach for something tender how to breach the cutting shards I washed my fingers at the sink the water always too cold Don’t cry. Don’t make it bigger. Smile. I tried doing what I was told In our home we kept the language only for what sounded fine the rest grew under the tongue at night like a thorn that won’t resign And the blackberries ripen and the blackberries teach the hearts how to reach for something tender how to breach the cutting shards My mother said it was my blame There was truth in her patient tone “If you hadn’t—” “If you didn’t—” “If you would’ve just left it alone.” It made my fear a household chore my tears a mess to mop and when I broke into myself They asked why I couldn’t stop And the blackberries ripen and the blackberries teach the hearts how to reach for something tender how to breach the cutting shards Don’t go into the bramble bush See what you did? Now look he’s angry because you pushed him You brought out the thorn, you shook Don’t go into the bramble bush It wasn’t about the yard It meant: don’t ask, don’t need, don’t cry Just please don’t make it hard Don’t go into the bramble bush Keep your voice low. Keep it sweet Don’t bring a problem to the kitchen Don’t bleed where they can see I learned how to harvest quietly palms open, skin unbraced I laughed too loud when the bramble caught my sleeve like a familiar place I filled a bowl with dark planets offered them up on a white plate later swallowed the metal taste of every word I didn’t make Don’t go into the bramble bush It grows inside your chest a rule that says your safety depends on being less Now I go back differently Not for a fruit, for what's true I cut a narrow path through the thicket let light do what the light can do I teach my kids other trails where hands come home still whole where sweetness isn’t something earned by bleeding out your soul I let the blackberries ripen, I let the blackberries heal the land I choose a kinder summer where no cut pays for love in hand I found the bramble in my thinking a soft command, a coiled refrain how quick my tongue can learn the weather how fast my love can turn to “same” It isn’t fate, but it is knowledge: what grows unchecked will spread its thread So I watch for vines in my voice now and pull them up before they’re fed I hold my children like a promise, not something owed, not something won I will not hand them down the tangle as if it’s all we’re living on I’ll name the dark, I’ll make it smaller, I’ll let their bright be loud and long, and when the bramble calls me homeward I’ll choose the path that breaks the song

Map I: Eulogy to Unborn

Fresh of the cart, he picks a stick. Eyes scan the view, no movement tricks. His ink is ash, sketches are quick. Dust settles slowly on yellowed bricks. He marks the roads, which violent gust Turned into holes, in sudden blast. He ticks the homes, whose loss of trust Banned inhabitants to barren crust. Dawn brings the light, noon brings the heat. Don't let the calm force mind to cheat. Playgrounds are empty, no happy feet Run laughing wildly, to their heart beat. Dusk throws the shadows, night - welcome breeze. From rubble pile, wind T-shirt frees. A memoir found of life at ease, The smallest handprint makes his step freeze. No border guards, no marching men Will read the maps he draws for them. But still he marks, with measured hand, The vanished paths of ravaged sand. The map folds once, then once again— A coffin big enough for ten.

Map II: Road That Only Goes Out

He marks the track through fruitful lands Heavy with weight of leaving hands Its origin—a roofless square Its end is bare, for none go there No arrows point the homeward way No milestones count the backward day Each bend erases what was near The ink runs thin, the road unclear He charts the ditch where tears had dried The shoes that fell from smaller stride A necklace lost in mud in haste When fearful ran away while chased He draws a camp spot under bridge With cold stone beds now left besmidged He traces all of the remains Scattered en route like empty grains With careful strokes he draws a home A place of refuge some call their own This temporary breathing space Will not make the past erased The road grows pale, then disappears A thread unspooling into years The map’s last mark is void throughout The road that only goes out

When the sky falls

it does not shatter at once it loosens blue by blue until even the birds forget what held them When the sky falls it lands in ordinary places on the plate left in the sink on the car cooling in the garage on the silence between people who meant to be wise When the sky falls it is slow enough for everyone to notice and still too late the dogs will whine at nothing the elders will remember quietly When the sky falls it does not smell of sulphur but of burning plastic and the trees for once do not know what to do When the sky falls it comes without mercy not as fire not as flood but as a weight pressing its blue knee against our throats When the sky has fallen we will not ask for rescue we will laugh like the frantic we will know, by then, the heavens have opened not to forgive us but to loose at us what we have always feared was already riding among us

Paintbox

Tis full of promise, when you bring it home. All pigments are new, cast in pans alone. Plethora of colors, all yours to own. They will not blend, if you dream and postpone. You open the lid, the world feels so wide. Each shade an option, not yet denied. You test with a brush, unsure where to start. The brightest run thin, the darkest feel sharp. Some hues you waste, some you never explore. And space in the box grows less than before. Choices dwindle, pans run dry. Colors mix, time fades the dye. In the sea once you could drown, Now what's left is shades of brown. The box held a world so bright. Echoes serve the failing sight. A short line of muted tone, Final breath in monochrome.

October Readme

I am a sum of four. I am a thinker, a tinker, a reader, and storyteller. I am a child of transition. My eyes and hair bear a kiss of the sun, a memory of summer turning to autumn. My freckles and lines build a map of my life, woken by the golden light of October, basked in the joy, celebration, respite, and appreciation. I am a human, a woman, a goddess of fertility, an alluring alloy of strength and fragility. I am a philosopher, an ethnographer, a scientist, and an archivist. I hide behind the faces of Svetovit and Mokosh, of Perun and Weles, keepers of the old, harbingers of the new. My life counted not in years but cycles. My superpower: vitality, drawn from the only mother we know. I am a creator, a labourer, a rebel, a protector, driven by curiosity, fear, shame and love. Untamed, but limited by freedom of others and time. My tears and memories are fossilized like insects in amber; I wear the weight of millennia on my shoulders.
