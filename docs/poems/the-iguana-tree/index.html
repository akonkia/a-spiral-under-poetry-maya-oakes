The iguana tree

Alek came wearing a black coat covered all over in seeds. We kissed him, we held him, laughed like no time had passed, and he told us about a tree growing behind his house, yellow as sunlight through green glass The green iguanas loved that tree, he said. They came just to sit in it. Nothing more. They sat among its branches as if sitting there were enough. A few mornings later I woke with something moving over my skin. Tiny feet. Tiny tails. A swarm of little iguanas resting on my arms, my stomach, my shoulders, walking from hand to hand. They liked me I tried to gather but they kept moving One climbed my wrist one disappeared beneath the sheet, one snugged against my collarbone. I put them in the bathtub and watched them explore happily I did not know what they ate. So I walked to the pharmacy. The pharmacy was full of people. I chose a line. Another line grew behind me. I waited. My iguanas were waiting. Then a guard came over and asked, Where is your ticket? What ticket? How did you come here? I walked. No. How did you come here? I walked to the pharmacy He told me I had to leave. There was a system now. There had apparently always been a system except yesterday when there wasn't I said it was stupid. Outside, a woman followed me. Why are you being a problem? Am I the problem? I kept walking. My iguanas were waiting. Farther into the city I found a pet shop. The queue was smaller there. The room was quieter. Surely someone here would know what an iguana eats. Surely I could buy something, carry it home where the iguanas sit Then a bell rang. I thought: a break. Instead the walls began to move. Shelves, counter, customers, bags of food and cages, all sliding onto rails. The whole pet shop pulled into traffic. Everyone took hold of a pole as if this happened every day. So I held on too. We passed through the city waiting to arrive somewhere so the shop could become a shop again. And all I could think was: my iguanas, waiting at home I could stop counting them. I could stop asking how to keep them. I could let them climb. I could let them sit Where is the iguana tree? Where is the iguana tree? I could let the tree do what I could not
