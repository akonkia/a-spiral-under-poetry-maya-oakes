Where forest green turns dark to black
Where leaning trees muffle world's clack
There in the ancient woodlands' heart
There dwells the Leshy, tall and wild
His beard is moss, his hair is vine
His eyes like pools where moonlight shines
He keeps the forest fierce yet fair
All creatures swarm under his care
One day a hunter, young and bold
Entered the woods despite the cold
Old huntsmen's words he had belittled
Of votive gifts the spirits cheated
“All game is mine,” he laughed with scorn
“The greatest bowman now is born."
The Leshy watched, unseen, unheard
And called the fox with silent word
The hunter chased, but paths would bend
Each trail betrayed him, without an end
Days turned to nights, all arrows spent
His stomach growling, his body bent
At last he fell beneath an oak
With choking throat last prayer he spoke
The Leshy came, in shifting guise,
With storm-cloud brow and gleaming eyes
“Man of the bow, do you now see?
The forest lives, not just for thee.
Value the life that has been given,
Take what you must, and be forgiven."
With gentle hand he showed the way
And led him back at break of day
Returning home the hunter wept
His pride undone, his hubris swept
From then he walked with humbler tread
Thanking the lives that kept him fed
The Leshy's words are not forgotten
Straightening those by greed besotten
On windy nights from forest deep
Come cries for lives he cannot keep
